# Heimstrecke – reference

The long reference of the repository: layout, building worlds, running, screenshots, desktop build. The short entry point is the [README](../README.md); how a world is made is in [world-creation.md](./world-creation.md).

A racing game on real roads: pick an area, plan a route like in a map app, and drive it. Roads, land cover, buildings and trees come from OpenStreetMap, terrain from the 1 m DGM1 terrain model of Baden-Württemberg (LGL) or, in France, the 1 m LiDAR HD terrain model of the IGN, the far horizon from the Copernicus DEM. Stylised graphics first, textures later.

Status: feasibility spikes, see [spikes/2026-10-20_19-45_prototype-spikes.md](./spikes/2026-10-20_19-45_prototype-spikes.md).

## Layout

- `./worlds/` – one directory per world in git: the recipe `<id>/world.json` with its route and start points (`./worlds/stuttgart/` has `route.json` and `starts.json`), `./worlds/berglen/` and `./worlds/crailsheim/` (10 km worlds with start points, no route yet; the route between them follows), `./worlds/kirchheim/` (10 km around Kirchheim unter Teck with Ötlingen and the Talwald, DGM1, start points, a 14.9 km loop Ötlingen, Talwald forest tracks, Kirchheim and back as `route.json`, made with `tools/route_from_extract.py` from `route_waypoints.json`; 135 MB built, 57 MB packed), `./worlds/bavella/` (16 km in Corsica from the Col de Bavella down the D 268 to Solenzara and the coast, LiDAR HD terrain, the sea and the mediterranean biome, start points, no route; 284 MB built, 2.8 million trees and shrubs), `./worlds/korsika-aleria-corte/` (a 41 x 7 km strip along the T 50 from Corte to Aléria, turned to the road with `heading_deg`, LiDAR HD, mediterranean, start points along the T 50, no route), `./worlds/zypern-ayia-napa/` (10 km around Ayia Napa in Cyprus, UTM zone 36 via `epsg`, GEDTM30 terrain, the sea and the `mediterranean-dry` biome, a 7.0 km route from west of the marina along Nissi Avenue into the centre from `route_waypoints.json`, start points; 67 MB built, 23 MB packed), the world's smoke-test runs `<id>/smoke.json` (Stuttgart: tunnel and bridge), the clipping ratchet `<id>/clipping_baseline.json`, the scout scenes `<id>/scenes.json`, and `world.schema.json`, the JSON Schema of the packaged manifest
- `./pipeline/` – Python, run with `uv`: `fetch.py` (DGM1 or LiDAR HD download → one GeoTIFF), `extract.py` (PBF → cached bbox extract), `build.py` (recipe + extract + DGM1 + Copernicus → 1 km glTF tiles, horizon, `world.json` manifest), `pack.py` (built world → one `.world` package); modules `world.py` (loads and checks a world recipe, derives the Make variables of a world, defines the recipe keys of the manifest), `frame.py` (local frame), `terrain.py` (height sources), `sea.py` (the sea from the OSM coastline), `biome.py` (colours, rock and tree kinds per biome), `roads.py` (road graph, strips and junction surfaces), `conform.py` (terrain conformed to the roads), `bridges.py` (bridge decks lifted over the roads below, deck classes, abutments), `tunnels.py` (tunnel tubes and portal cuts), `sidewalks.py` (sidewalks and kerbs), `skirts.py` (skirts under the road and sidewalk edges), `islands.py` (holes between junction carriageways filled flat at road height), `priority.py` (the drawn carriageway in one index, so verges yield where they would lie on another road), `markings.py` (dashed lane lines and edge lines), `rails.py` (railway beds and rails; on a road only the rails), `trees.py` (forest, single trees and tree rows as per-tile instance files), `structures.py` (quad helpers shared by the bridge and tunnel layers), `starts.py` (start points snapped onto the roads), `metrics.py` (per-build report), `clipping.py` (clipping report and its viewpoints), `scout.py` (low cameras by street name)
- `./godot/` – Godot 4.7 project (GDScript, Jolt physics): `scenes/select.tscn` world selection (the main scene, `scripts/world_select.gd`), `scenes/fly.tscn` free camera, `scenes/drive.tscn` start menu with a map, then the car; `scripts/world_loader.gd` opens a world directory or `.world` package, finds the worlds and reads the launcher arguments; `tests/drive_smoke.gd` the headless smoke test with the timing reference `tests/reference_stuttgart.json` (`cars.gd` the four cars of the start menu, `car.gd` physics and drivetrain, `car_model.gd` procedural bodies, `car_audio.gd` synthesised engine sounds); `scripts/trees.gd` draws the trees in two levels of detail with trunk collision; `scripts/road_index.gd` holds the road centre lines (`roads.bin`) for F
- `./render/` – screenshots without a GPU: render image (`Dockerfile`, `entrypoint.sh`), host runner `render.sh`; the curated viewpoints live in `./godot/tools/viewpoints.json`, the low scout views in `./godot/tools/viewpoints_polish.json`, curve signs in Kirchheim and on the Aléria – Corte stage in `./godot/tools/viewpoints_curve_signs.json` and `./godot/tools/viewpoints_curve_signs_korsika.json` (with `RENDER_WORLD` of that world), the route start, three junctions on the route and one view off it in the Kirchheim world in `./godot/tools/viewpoints_route.json` (`RENDER_WORLD=data/out/worlds/kirchheim RENDER_VIEWS=res://tools/viewpoints_route.json`, for the route chevrons and boards), four overviews of the Bavella world in `./godot/tools/viewpoints_bavella.json` (`RENDER_WORLD=data/out/worlds/bavella RENDER_VIEWS=res://tools/viewpoints_bavella.json`), the Street View comparison spot in Solenzara in `./godot/tools/viewpoints_streetview_bavella.json`, Stadtbahn on its bed at the Pragsattel and two runs on a road in Stuttgart in `./godot/tools/viewpoints_rails_stuttgart.json`, route ends, Nissi Beach, the town and the coast of the Cyprus world in `./godot/tools/viewpoints_zypern_ayia_napa.json`; `./godot/tools/render_cars.gd` renders front, rear and side views of every car on a grey floor, without world data
- `./data/` – downloads (`raw/`, shared by all worlds) and generated output (`out/`: one `out/worlds/<id>/` per world with its extract, tiles, horizon, `world.json` and metrics, the packages in `out/packages/`), not in git

Requirements: `uv` 0.13 and Godot 4.7, for example via `mise use -g uv@0.13.1 godot@4.7.2-stable`.

## Build a world (default world: Stuttgart, stadium, city centre, Killesberg)

The short way is `make`: `make dev` downloads what is missing, builds the extract and the world and starts the drive scene; `make drive` alone rebuilds only what is older than its inputs, so after a pull that changed `pipeline/` it rebuilds the world before the game starts. Every step prints a header, a live elapsed-time line on a terminal and the result with its duration (`tools/step.sh`). `make help` lists all targets. Every world target takes `W=<id>` (default `W=stuttgart`); the targets are file targets, so a second `make world` with unchanged inputs rebuilds nothing. `make rebuild-world` forces extract and world, `make clean` deletes `data/out/worlds/<id>/` (downloads stay). Tools can be overridden, for example `make drive GODOT=godot4`; an existing regional extract such as `data/raw/stuttgart-regbez*.osm.pbf` is reused under either name.

```bash
make dev      # fresh checkout: download (about 750 MB), extract, world, drive
make drive    # start the game, rebuilding the world first if needed
make fly      # free camera
make test     # headless smoke test
make world W=<id>     # build another world into data/out/worlds/<id>/
make download W=<id>  # fetch only what that world is missing
make pack             # data/out/packages/stuttgart-1.world
```

A world is described by a recipe in git, `worlds/<id>/world.json` (schema 1): `id` (`[a-z0-9-]+`), `name`, `version` of the world data, `min_game` (the lowest game version that can load it, one to three numeric parts such as `1`, `0.1` or `0.1.0`), `center` (lat, lon), `size_km` (the playable square), `horizon_km` (the side of the horizon square), `bbox` of the OSM extract (west, south, east, north), `osm_region` (the Geofabrik path, for example `europe/germany/baden-wuerttemberg/stuttgart-regbez`), optionally `route` and `starts` (file names relative to the recipe) `width_km` and `heading_deg` (a rectangular world turned along a road, see below), `terrain` (`dgm1`, the default, or `lidarhd`), `biome` (`temperate`, the default, or `mediterranean`) and `tracks` (OSM tracktypes of `highway=track` ways driven as gravel roads, for example `["grade1", "grade2"]` in Berglen, see [world-creation.md](./world-creation.md#tracks)), the data `sources` (name, licence, url, credit) and the `attribution` sentence. A new world is a new recipe directory and nothing else: `make world W=<id>` builds it into `data/out/worlds/<id>/`, the extract included, because the extract depends on the bbox. The downloads are derived from the recipe (`pipeline/world.py vars`, one call per make run): the Copernicus cells the horizon square touches, the name of the DGM1 GeoTIFF from centre and size, and the Geofabrik region with its URL; `make download W=<id>` fetches what is missing and skips what exists. The LGL download for a 10 km world is about 450 MB, and DGM1 covers Baden-Württemberg only. For France the recipe sets `"terrain": "lidarhd"`: `fetch.py --source lidarhd` takes the MNT LiDAR HD of the IGN (1 m, Licence Ouverte 2.0) from the Géoplateforme WMS as raw float32 GeoTIFF blocks of 4 km in Lambert-93 (EPSG:2154, cached under `data/raw/lidarhd/blocks/`, 64 MB each, 25 blocks and about 1.6 GB for the 16 km Bavella world) and warps them bilinearly onto the 1 m EPSG:25832 grid, so everything after the download sees the same raster as with DGM1. The service marks the sea as no data and resamples that into the neighbouring cells, so everything below -5 m counts as no data. The sea comes from the OSM coastline (`sea.py`): `extract.py` keeps every `natural=coastline` way that crosses the bbox, the build cuts the raster square along them and keeps the pieces right of the nearest coastline (OSM draws the land on the left). Terrain cells in the sea get at most 0 m, cells without data up to 30 m landward of the coastline 0 m as well (the edge of the LiDAR data is not the shore line); the sea counts as water land cover, so the terrain is drawn blue and no trees stand in it, and horizon vertices at 0 m are drawn as water; from the water stretches of the horizon border a flat sea reaches 25 km further out, because the camera sees 40 km and the sea would otherwise end at the horizon square. Cells without data farther inland stay without data, and the build stops there as before.

A world does not have to be a square facing north. With `width_km` (default `size_km`) and `heading_deg` (default 0, counter-clockwise from east, -90 to 90) it is a rectangle of `size_km` along the heading by `width_km` across it, and the local frame turns with it (`frame.py`): local X points along the heading, -Z 90° left of it, so everything that works in local metres is unchanged. The square assumptions are pairs now: `Tiles`, the conform grid (rows along y, columns along x), the road clip, trees, metrics, clipping and the bridge report take the half side as a scalar or as `[hx, hy]`; Stuttgart and Bavella build byte-identical tiles and tree files. `Dgm1Raster` checks the coverage at the four turned corners and reads rotated rasters through `frame.to_utm()`; `fetch.py --width --heading` (from the Makefile) warps LiDAR HD onto a 1 m grid along the local axes (a rotated geotransform, `lidarhd_<E>_<N>_<L>x<W>_h<heading>.tif`) and fetches only the WMS blocks that touch the area. A turned world needs LiDAR HD, DGM1 stays on the square grid. The manifest carries `width_km` and `heading_deg` only for such a world. The Corte–Aléria strip takes its heading (-34.3°) from a line fitted through the T 50 nodes of the OSM extract; the T 50 lies at most 2.2 km off that axis, so 7 km leave at least 1.3 km on either side. In Godot the drive menu's map shows the strip along its local axes, not north up, and the sun stands in the local frame as before, so its compass direction turns with the world.

The recipe key `biome` sets the look of the landscape (`pipeline/biome.py`). `temperate`, the default, is the look of the Baden-Württemberg worlds, unchanged (the Stuttgart tiles and tree files are byte-identical). `mediterranean` paints the ground, meadows, fields and horizon drier and yellower, scrub olive, forest floor darker, beaches sand-coloured, and blends in a warm granite grey on OSM bare rock and scree, between 1150 and 1550 m above sea level and on slopes steeper than 0.75 to 1.2 (rise over run), water excepted; the horizon takes the grey by height. `extract.py` now keeps `natural=bare_rock`, `rock`, `stone`, `scree` and `cliff` as `rock`, `beach`, `sand` and `shingle` as `sand` and `natural=scrub` and `heath` as `scrub` (in `temperate` drawn in the meadow colour as before; rock and sand have no colour there and are not painted). The trees of a mediterranean world have four kinds: holm oak (`oak`, 7-14 m, dark round crown), pine (`pine`, 14-26 m, a bare trunk under a flat umbrella crown, standing for the maritime, laricio and stone pines), cypress (`cypress`, a narrow spindle, for mapped `genus=Cupressus`) and shrub (`shrub`, 1.2-3 m, a low dome, the maquis). The conifer share of a forest becomes its pine share; untagged forests get 35 % pines up to 500 m and 85 % from 1000 m, holm oak below. Scrub outside forest and rock gets a shrub every 8 m (jittered, 6 % of them holm oaks of 4-9 m) from its own RNG, so the other trees are the same as without the maquis; shrubs get no collision trunk and `trees.gd` draws them near only up to 220 m and far up to 1200 m. Houses in a mediterranean world get yellow ochre (30 %), peach (25 %), cream (25 %), pink (10 %) and granite (10 %) facades by the same centroid hash as the temperate plaster mix, terracotta roofs (the roof vertices come first in `extrude()`), hipped where the footprint is nearly a rectangle up to 16 m wide, and untagged houses two to four storeys (`biome.py`, Street View comparison of 2026-11-04); a house outside the `urban` land cover with fewer than 3 other buildings within 60 m and without a `height` or `building:levels` tag has the ground floor only, 3.5 m instead of the 7-9 m default (Bavella 198 of 2729, Corte–Aléria 1310 of 7919). Bavella has about 630 000 shrubs next to 2.1 million forest trees; the software render needs `RENDER_MEMORY=5g` for it (peak 3.4 GB), the default limit stays 3g. The build holds the whole area in memory, so areas much larger than 10 km are built on a developer machine, not on a shared host.

The build writes the manifest `data/out/worlds/<id>/world.json` (schema 1): `schema`, `id`, `name`, `version`, `min_game`, then the build's keys (`center`, `size_km`, `tile_m`, `base_height`, `tiles`, `horizon`, `route`, `terrain_source`, `terrain_file`, `horizon_source`, and `waypoints` and `starts` when the recipe has them), then `sources` and `attribution`. `worlds/world.schema.json` (JSON Schema draft 2020-12) is the single definition of the packaged manifest, shared by `pack`, the game's loader and a later launcher. The loader checks only `schema`, `min_game` (compared numerically, part by part, with `application/config/version` in `godot/project.godot`, today `0.1.0`; missing parts count as 0) and the keys it needs at run time; `pack` validates the full manifest and writes nothing if it fails.

`make pack W=<id>` packs a built world into one shareable file, `data/out/packages/<id>-<version>.world`: a ZIP (deflate) with all entries at the root, `world.json` first, then `horizon.glb` and the tiles. Because the manifest comes first, any ZIP tool or Godot's `ZIPReader` reads it without touching the tiles. The packaged `world.json` adds `built` (UTC), `commit` (short git commit of the pipeline), `size_bytes` (the uncompressed files) and `files` (the SHA-256 of every entry), and carries `sources` and `attribution` like the directory manifest. Routes and start points travel inside the manifest, so a package needs no file next to it; the smoke-test data (`worlds/<id>/smoke.json` and the reference timings) stay in the repository because they belong to the test, not to playing. Raw data, the extract and the metrics are never packed. A package may be at most 500 MB, `pack` aborts above that (`--max-bytes`); Stuttgart packs 175.6 MB into 56.6 MB. Loading from a package costs about 3 % more than from the directory (smoke test on the shared host: 7.9 s against 7.6 s).

The steps behind `make dev` for Stuttgart (the values are what `pipeline/world.py vars worlds/stuttgart/world.json` derives):

DGM1 (LGL, dl-de/by-2-0, about 450 MB download for the 10 km world) is the terrain source for the ground, roads, buildings and route; the Copernicus tiles only draw the horizon ring. `fetch.py` converts the DGM1 zips into one GeoTIFF and deletes the zips and text files afterwards.

```bash
mkdir -p data/raw && cd data/raw
curl -sSLO https://download.geofabrik.de/europe/germany/baden-wuerttemberg/stuttgart-regbez-latest.osm.pbf
curl -sSL -o cop30_N48_E009.tif \
  https://copernicus-dem-30m.s3.amazonaws.com/Copernicus_DSM_COG_10_N48_00_E009_00_DEM/Copernicus_DSM_COG_10_N48_00_E009_00_DEM.tif
curl -sSL -o cop30_N48_E008.tif \
  https://copernicus-dem-30m.s3.amazonaws.com/Copernicus_DSM_COG_10_N48_00_E008_00_DEM/Copernicus_DSM_COG_10_N48_00_E008_00_DEM.tif
cd ../..
uv run python pipeline/fetch.py --center 48.79,9.2 --size 10 --out data/raw
uv run python pipeline/extract.py data/raw/stuttgart-regbez-latest.osm.pbf \
  --bbox 9.125,48.74,9.275,48.84 --out data/out/worlds/stuttgart/extract.pkl
uv run python pipeline/build.py data/out/worlds/stuttgart/extract.pkl \
  --terrain data/raw/dgm1/dgm1_509_5398_12.tif \
  --horizon-dem data/raw/cop30_N48_E009.tif data/raw/cop30_N48_E008.tif \
  --recipe worlds/stuttgart/world.json \
  --out data/out/worlds/stuttgart
uv run python pipeline/pack.py --recipe worlds/stuttgart/world.json \
  --world data/out/worlds/stuttgart --out data/out/packages
```

Every build writes `metrics.json` and `metrics.md` next to the tiles. Four gates are hard (the build exits with code 3 if one fails): all road vertices at a shared node have the same height in the exported tiles; the conformed terrain stays below every road strip (at least 2 cm under the surface at strip centre and edges, checked between the points the conform pass itself uses; bridges and tunnels excluded); the largest grade of the drawn road surface is at most 25 %; and every ground node lies within 2 m of the DGM1 median around it. Connectivity, crossings, the slope table, road-to-DGM1 offsets, structures, bridges, tunnels, the route, seams, the budget and the clipping report are only reported; three informational gates (the largest component at least 99 % of the road length, the budget of at most 900 s build time and 400 MB tiles, clipping not above its baseline) are listed with the hard ones but never fail the build. The full Stuttgart world is about 197 MB of tiles (173.9 MB before the banks, edge lines and taper) and builds in about 60 s.

The hard gates prove that the surface is continuous and the terrain stays below every strip, but not that drawn pieces stay out of each other. `clipping.py` checks the drawn geometry for that and writes the `clipping` block of `metrics.json` and a Clipping section in `metrics.md`, every finding with kind, severity, way, name, position and road height. Kinds: `zfight` (two strips or a strip and a foreign junction surface overlapping in plan at the same height), `overlap` (the same, less than a crossing's height apart), `median` (two carriageways closer than two skirt runs, which must share the median), `width_step` (a notch where the half width jumps at a degree-2 node), `terrain_above` (terrain above a sidewalk's outer top edge or a corner), `bank_buried` (terrain above a bank's top; not measured since the banks were removed), `skirt_cross` (a skirt foot running over another road or crossing another skirt) and `dip` (a ground profile sagging into a pothole). The informational gate is a ratchet against `clipping_baseline.json` next to the world's recipe (`worlds/stuttgart/clipping_baseline.json`), the counts of origin/main before this work, for the recipe's centre and size: every kind with a measured baseline may exceed its count by 2 % (rounded up, because unrelated geometry changes shift a few overlaps across the thresholds), and the total of the measured kinds may not grow; kinds without a measured baseline are listed only, and a world without the file (or whose centre or size differ from those in it) reports SKIPPED. On the Stuttgart world `width_step` went from 1082 to 0 and the measured total from 3919 to 2790. For a new world, or to refresh the Stuttgart counts after a deliberate change, write `worlds/<id>/clipping_baseline.json` with its `center`, `size_km`, `date`, `commit` and per-kind `count`/`measured`, measured by a build of the reference commit with the current `clipping.py`; the fixture's `_comment` describes how the Stuttgart counts were measured. The build also writes the three worst findings per kind (at most 15) as `viewpoints.json` in the world's output directory (`data/out/worlds/stuttgart/viewpoints.json`), 25 m above and 40 m back along the road, ready for the render container, which mounts the world at `/work/world`: `RENDER_VIEWS=/work/world/viewpoints.json ./render/render.sh`.

Node heights are smoothed before the edges are sampled, because DGM1 is bare earth: at a bridge abutment or tunnel portal the 3 m median follows the ground edge rather than the road, so portal and abutment nodes take their height from DGM1 fitted 2-15 m from the node on the ground side; then node heights are relaxed to at most 22 % between neighbouring nodes (each ground node moving at most 2 m) and the drawn profiles are limited to 24 %. Only `bridge` and `tunnel` are structures (interpolated between their end nodes; tunnels and bridge decks are excluded from conforming, see below); building passages, culverts and covered ways are ground roads and follow the terrain. The route takes its heights from the drawn road surface at the nearest road segment, bridges and tunnels included, and falls back to the conformed terrain for points farther than 10 m from every road. OSRM points lie up to tens of metres apart, and a straight piece between two far points cuts through a crest or floats over a dip, so the build first resamples the route to at most 5 m (`ROAD_STEP`) between points, keeping the original ones; the waypoints stay as they are. The report shows both counts, `route.points` (dense, 4216 for the fixture) and `route.source_points` (from OSRM, 1041). `route_marks.gd` draws the chosen route as red chevrons on the road, pointing the way to drive: a strip 2 m wide and 0.08 m above the route points with a chevron texture repeating every 4 m, unshaded and visible from both sides, because Godot draws line primitives one pixel wide and a line vanishes at speed; 0.08 m lies above the markings (0.02 m) and on bridge decks, and the tunnel floor lies below the road surface. The strip has no collision. Every road that leaves the route (a side road, the arms of a crossing, the road straight on where the route turns) gets a barrier across it, 7 m wide, facing the junction. Only the road straight on where the route turns (leaving within about 35° of the way the route came in) gets a red and white chevron board on two posts, its chevrons pointing where the route goes (the route point 25 m beyond the junction); the chevrons are red geometry 2 cm in front of the white board, not a texture, because fine texture stripes shimmer on the GPU when the camera moves. Every other arm gets two layers of square hay bales (2.35 × 0.9 × 1.2 m, three below, two above). The barriers of one junction (within 32 m of each other) are joined by ads with made-up oil and tyre brands (white on green, yellow on blue, white on red, ...), one panel per 3.4 m, the brand shrunk to fit: between two barriers next to each other around the junction a circular arc (a quarter circle at a right-angled turn) from the end of one to the end of the other, except where the route itself runs through, so the junction is fenced off; the arcs take turns between banners on a fence and perimeter boards leaning back on the ground; a road shorter than 60 m with another board close by gets no board, the barrier fences it in. `make test-gates` checks the arcs on a synthetic junction. The arms come from `roads.bin`: an edge with half its samples within 3 m of the route is part of it, any other with an end within 15 m (and 4 m in height, so not a bridge over it) that lies within 3 m or points at the route is an arm, because the drawn road ends are cut back at junctions; the board stands where the arm is 7 m from the route. Kirchheim gets 86 boards, Stuttgart 132. In the drive scene the boards collide (buildings layer), so the closed roads cannot be taken. Before every curve of the route stand pace note signs (`signs.gd` with the kinds `pace`, `pace_sharp`, `pace_tight`, a framed panel (coloured on the front only, back and edges grey like the posts, as on every sign) 2.0 × 3.3 m with its bottom 1 m above the road), upright: the grade from 1 to 6, H for a hairpin, large above a bent arrow (geometry, `@curve_right` / `@curve_left` lines in `signs.gd`) the way the curve goes; white for grades 4 to 6, yellow for 2 and 3, red for 1 and hairpin. A curve that runs straight into the next shows both pairs side by side on one sign, in driving order, coloured by the tighter. Every curve gets two signs, 100 and 50 m before it, both 6 m from the route on the outside of the curve (left of a right curve, right of a left curve). A sign stands at least 10 m past the previous curve (the nearest moves closer, the others are left out) and 15 m before its own, else there is none. Signs have no collision and may stand in a building or behind a direction sign in a town street. The chevron band on the road is off at first; G shows or hides it (in the drive and the fly scene), because it helps in towns, where the route turns at junctions, more than on a country road. The signs come from the route's pace notes (`pace_notes.gd`), the notes a rally co-driver reads, in preparation for a co-driver who calls them: every curve with its direction, a grade from 6 (fastest) to 1 (slowest) by its smallest radius (below 400, 140, 90, 60, 40 and 25 m; under 15 m and turning at least 135° a hairpin), the modifiers long (50 m and 90° or more), tightens and opens (the last third against the first, 0.7), and the link to the next curve: into (up to 25 m of straight), and (up to 60 m) or the distance (50, 80, 100, 150, ... 1000 m, rounded down). The curvature is the heading change over 15 m on either side of a 5 m resample, so a polyline corner at a junction reads as a turn of about 20 m radius (grade 1); a route running to a waypoint and back (170° within 20 m) has no note. Kirchheim has 68 notes (34 of them grade 1, mostly junction turns), Aléria – Corte 111 (mostly 4 to 6). `make test-notes` checks the analysis on drawn routes (`godot/tests/pace_notes_test.gd`). A call reads grade before direction as on the sign: `3 left open`, `4 right into 5 left`, `hairpin left, 150` (`note.words` are the words, `note.call` the text). `co_driver.gd` calls them in the drive scene: a curve is called when it is 6 s ahead at the current speed (at least 80 m, at most 500 m), curves linked by into or and are one call, a passed curve is not called, and a jump along the route (R, F, a new start) starts over from the car's place; M mutes it. The voice is one clip per word or phrase (`CoDriver.clip_words()`: 1 to 6, hairpin, left, right, long, tightens, open, into, and, the distances 50 to 1000, each grade with its direction as `4_right` or `hairpin_left`, and that pair after a link as `into_3_left` or `and_5_right`), read as `<name>.wav` or `.ogg` from `user://codriver/` (a voice of one's own, taken first) or `res://audio/codriver/` without an import and played 6 dB louder on the bus `CoDriver` (a radio: 400 to 3200 Hz, a little distortion). A call plays the longest phrase that has a clip, up to three words, because a grade and its direction said one after the other in single words sound chopped; a word without a clip is skipped. The game's 69 clips (about 0.6 MB) are made by `make voice` (`tools/codriver_voice.py`): Piper with the voice `en_US-norman-medium` (public domain data, see [licences.md](./licences.md#co-driver-voice)), each word synthesised with a trailing comma (Piper babbles on after a bare single word), at length scale 0.85 (0.7 for long) and noise 0.333, trimmed to its sound plus 30 ms, faded out over 40 ms, normalised to -1 dBFS, a distance with 180 ms of silence in front for the comma of `hairpin left, 150`. Each clip has an `.import` file with the importer `keep`, so the editor leaves it alone and an export carries the file itself for `FileAccess` (checked with `--export-pack`: all clips in the PCK). Piper's noise makes every run sound a little different, so regenerate only to change the voice and listen to the result. The co-driver comes with the route and the car, so in a world without a route (Berglen, Crailsheim: free drive only) there is none. `make test-codriver` checks when a call is due, which clips a call plays and that every clip of `clip_words()` is in `res://audio/codriver/` (`godot/tests/co_driver_test.gd`). `build.py` checks the tag list an extract was made with: an `extract.pkl` made before `covered` was recorded makes it exit with code 2, re-run `extract.py` then.

The terrain follows the roads, not the other way round: up to 15 m beyond a strip edge the 10 m terrain grid takes the ground height of the nearest road, then blends back to DGM1 over 25 m; a last pass lowers single grid triangles where a bend, a grade change or a second road would still let terrain through; it pushes at fixed points along the strip centre and edges and where these lines cross a grid line or a triangle diagonal of the mesh, where the piecewise-linear terrain rises highest over the road (without those, a residential road in Corte lost its sidewalk points and kept 1 cm instead of 2 cm; now the smallest clearance is 0.11 to 0.14 m in all worlds). Roads are drawn 0.1 m above that ground (plus a few cm per road class against z-fighting), so leaving the road is no longer a step. Over tunnels the terrain stays DGM1: a grid point nearer to a tunnel than to the nearest ground road is not conformed, so a ground road ending at a portal no longer digs into the hill above the tunnel. Under bridge decks the same rule keeps DGM1, so the ground roads at an abutment no longer fill the valley under the bridge start. The Stuttgart route starts on Benzstraße at the stadium because the previous start in the stadium parking area was on service roads, which are not drivable.

Where the lane count changes at a degree-2 node (a tag split without a junction surface, for example the Paulinenbrücke ramp going from two to three lanes), the two strips used to meet with a notch of up to a few metres. Now both blend to the mean half width of the two over `TAPER` (30 m, at most half the drawn edge, `roads.taper_half`), so the edge runs on without a step; every reader of the strip width (sidewalks, skirts, markings, clipping) takes the half width per road sample, `net.half`, and the lane lines follow as fractions of it. The report lists `taper_nodes` (1282 in Stuttgart) and `taper_max_step_m`.

Ground roads lie between sidewalks (`sidewalks.py`): each side with a sidewalk gets a 1.8 m strip on a 0.12 m kerb, so the road sits kerb-deep between them. The sides come from the OSM tags `sidewalk`, `sidewalk:both`, `sidewalk:left` and `sidewalk:right` (`separate` sidewalks, mapped as their own way, are drawn next to the road all the same); untagged residential, unclassified, tertiary, secondary and primary roads get both in town (at least half of the edge in urban land cover or with two buildings within 40 m; outside towns an untagged road has none, so the country roads of Berglen and Corsica have no sidewalks), other classes and links none, and neither do bridges, tunnels and the ground roads ending at a tunnel portal. On a oneway carriageway, often one half of a dual road, `separate` and the default only count on the outer side (the driver's right), so no sidewalk runs along the median. At a junction, two neighbouring arms with sidewalks on the sides facing each other get a corner piece from the junction edge out to where their outer lines meet (cut off straight at sharp angles), with its own kerb; the skirt runs round its outer line. The terrain cannot rise to the sidewalk top: it is a 10 m grid, and every triangle reaching under the road is pushed below the road ground, so beyond a sidewalk it lies about 0.3 m below its top. A skirt (`skirts.py`) closes that step: from every ground road edge, or the sidewalk's outer edge on a side with one, and from each junction or corner outline it runs outward and down at 20 degrees below the horizontal until it ends 0.1 m under the conformed terrain. Nothing rises above the edge; the bank that used to run 1.0 m outward and 0.25 m up in front of the skirt read as a raised kerb on open land and was removed on 2026-10-25. A skirt reaches out at most 3 m, so where a ramp runs beside a lower road and the terrain follows the lower one (up to 16 m below, at the Charlottenplatz and Pragstraße junctions), it steepens into a retaining wall instead of reaching over the road below. The skirt is drawn in a light stone colour (`COLORS["bank"]`), because the former dark grey read as a shadow or a seam and stone reads as a kerb. The road has priority (`priority.py`): every drawn ground carriageway piece (strip segments and junction surfaces) sits in one index, and skirts, sidewalk segments and corners that would lie on another road yield (their own arms and junction surface excluded), because a kerb on a carriageway is a step a car runs into and a skirt there pokes through the asphalt. Between two carriageways closer than two skirt runs, a narrow median, the skirt of each side reaches at most half the free gap and steepens there, so two skirts do not cross in the median. None of this can come from the terrain itself: it is a 10 m grid and cannot follow a 3 m median. Sidewalks, kerbs and skirts form the `verges` layer, which collides; the angle is `ANGLE` in `skirts.py`.

Main roads (tertiary to trunk) carry dashed lines of 3 m every 9 m (`markings.py`): a centre line on two-way roads at least 6 m wide, a line between the lanes of a oneway carriageway with a `lanes` tag. They sit 2 cm above the surface in their own `markings` layer, which the drive scene loads without collision. Without them the flat-shaded road gives the eye nothing that streams past, and speed reads far lower than it is. The dash pattern runs on through degree-2 nodes instead of restarting at every tag split. Secondary, primary and trunk roads also get a continuous edge line, 0.15 m wide and 0.25 m inside both drawn edges, which follows a width taper and frames the carriageway against the light skirts. Neither dashes nor edge lines are drawn on junction surfaces, where they would cross the other road. Buildings get one facade colour each, 60 % warm plaster tones, 20 % white and 20 % grey, chosen from the footprint centroid with an explicit integer mix (`build.facade_color`) in place of one beige shade varied by Python's `hash`, so the colours are the same in every build and the blocks read as separate houses.

Tunnels get their own mesh layer `tunnels` per tile (`tunnels.py`), loaded with collision like the others. Every 5 m segment of a tunnel edge is classified by the conformed terrain on the centre line and both wall lines (0.5 m beyond the strip edge): where it lies at least 4 m above the road surface at both ends, the segment gets a tube with walls and a ceiling at road + 5.5 m, lowered to 0.5 m under the terrain where the cover is thinner; where the terrain is only partly above the road (the cut in front of a portal), the terrain mesh is cut away between the wall lines and retaining walls run up to the terrain; where it is below the road, nothing is added. A portal face closes the gap between ceiling and terrain where a tube ends, wall pieces that would stand on a branching ramp are left out, and all faces have both windings because the material and the collision are one-sided. The drive scene places the car with a ray from 3 m above the start or route point, below the lowest ceiling, so points in tunnels land on the road.

Bridges get their own mesh layer `bridges` per tile (`bridges.py`), loaded with collision like the others. A bridge used to be a straight chord between its abutment nodes, which can sag far below the real deck, so first the deck is lifted where it crosses another road: wherever a bridge segment crosses a segment of an edge without a shared node and lies at least 1 m above it, the surface must clear that road by 4.5 m plus the 1.2 m deck; the requirement holds over the width of the road below and falls off at 8 %, nodes inside a bridge chain may rise as far as the 22 % node limit from the fixed abutment nodes allows, and the profile stays inside the 24 % cones of its node heights. Where that is not enough (a short bridge between two abutments), the ground road below gets a dip of the same shape; DGM1 under a bridge is interpolated anyway, because the laser does not see under the deck. OSM `layer` tags are not used. Then every 5 m segment of a bridge edge is classified by DGM1 on the centre line and both deck edge lines: a `deck` segment, whose underside lies at least 0.5 m above DGM1 at both ends, gets a deck body (walkway, parapet, outer faces, underside), and the terrain under it stays DGM1; every other segment is `low` (a short bridge over a brook or a path, a chord running into a slope), gets no body and is conformed like a ground road, so no deck sinks into the terrain and no body stands on it as a bulge. Deck runs shorter than 15 m between two abutments become `low`. Where a deck run ends, an end face closes the body down to 0.5 m below the terrain, and the conform pass pins the corners of every terrain triangle crossing the abutment line to the deck's ground plane, so the terrain meets the deck end without a step; where another road strip in the same 10 m terrain cells lies lower (a road below, a parallel carriageway, a grade change at the abutment), the push-down pass lowers them again, and `metrics.md` lists those abutments separately. Parapet pieces that would stand on another strip are left out, and all faces have both windings.

The recipe's `route` file may be any OSRM route response (`overview=full&geometries=geojson`) in place of the Stuttgart one.

Trees come from OSM as well (`trees.py`): forests (`natural=wood`, `landuse=forest`, about 280 areas around Stuttgart) are filled on a 7 m grid with up to 2.5 m jitter, their `leaf_type` sets the share of conifers (untagged 25 %); single trees (`natural=tree`, about 17,000 in the area) stand where they are mapped, tree rows (`natural=tree_row`) get one every 9 m, with `height`, `leaf_type` and `genus` where tagged. Forest trees keep 5.8 m from the road side and 2 m from buildings, mapped trees only leave the carriageway itself and buildings; none stand in water or the tunnel cuts. The positions are seeded, so a rebuild gives the same trees. Footways, tracks and railways are not in the extract, so forest trees may stand on them. Each tile gets `<tile>.trees` beside its glb (packed into the `.world` as well, listed under `trees` in `world.json`): float32 MultiMesh instances (transform and tint) the game assigns as a whole, without a loop per tree; `trees.gd` draws each 250 m chunk with trunk and crown up to 450 m and casting shadows, and each tile with a few triangles per tree up to 3.5 km. Mapped trees and forest trees within 15 m of a road side get a trunk cylinder on the buildings layer (`<tile>.trunks`), so the car hits them; the shapes go into one static body per tile before it enters the physics space, otherwise Jolt rebuilds the compound on every shape (4 s instead of 70 ms for about 40,000 trunks).

The recipe's `starts` file lists the start points of the drive menu (`worlds/stuttgart/starts.json`, 15 starts: stadium, city centre, Killesberg, TV tower, top of the Neue Weinsteige facing downhill, main station, Pragsattel, Bad Cannstatt, Wilhelma, Mercedes-Benz Museum, Untertürkheim, Bopser, Rotenwaldstraße, Botnang, Heslach; keys 1-9 and 0 choose the first ten, a click any of them). Each entry is snapped to the nearest drawn sample of the named road and faces `toward`; the entry with `route_start` uses the route's first point and direction and is the only one with the lap timer. The build prints the road and snap distance for every start.

## Run

`make drive`, `make fly` and `make test` run the drive, fly and smoke-test commands, after `make world` and with `--world` set to the world of `W`:

```bash
godot --path godot                           # world selection, then drive
godot --path godot res://scenes/fly.tscn -- --world data/out/worlds/stuttgart
godot --path godot -- --world data/out/packages/stuttgart-1.world --start 1
godot --path godot res://scenes/drive.tscn -- --world <directory or .world file>
godot --headless --path godot --script res://tests/drive_smoke.gd \
  -- --world data/out/packages/stuttgart-1.world
```

Launcher arguments go after `--` and are the stable interface of every entry point (the selection, the drive and fly scenes, the smoke test and `render_views.gd`): `--world <directory or .world file>` skips the selection and loads that world, a relative path taken relative to the directory Godot was started from; `--start <index>` skips the start menu and starts on that start point (an index out of range logs an error and opens the menu); `--route <id>` makes a user route the chosen route instead of the world's own (an unknown id logs an error and keeps the world's route). A world whose `min_game` is above the game's version is refused with the reason. `make drive`, `make fly` and `make test` pass `--world` for `W`, so they skip the selection.

Without `--world` the game opens the world selection (`scenes/select.tscn`, the main scene). It lists every world the loader finds, in this order: `user://worlds/` (`.world` files and directories with `world.json`), the repository's `data/out/worlds/*/` and `data/out/packages/*.world`, and every directory in `HEIMSTRECKE_WORLDS` (colon-separated). Each entry shows name, version, number of routes, size in MB and build time, whether it is a package or a directory, and its path; the size is `size_bytes`, the unpacked tiles and horizon (175.6 MB for Stuttgart, not the 56.6 MB file). A directory and a package of the same world are both listed, and a world that cannot be opened is listed disabled with the reason. The attribution of the highlighted world is shown at the bottom. Keys 1-9 and 0 or a click choose, Up/Down move, Enter/Space start. The chosen world starts the drive scene with its start menu as before; the menu's title is the world name, and name and attribution show at the bottom of the screen for five seconds after the first start. `user://` is Godot's per-user data directory, `~/.local/share/godot/app_userdata/Test Drive NT/` on Linux.

User routes live in `user://routes/<world id>/<id>.json` as `{"schema": 1, "name": "…", "points": [[x, y, z], …], "waypoints": [[x, y, z], …]}` in world metres, the same shape as `route` and `waypoints` in the manifest; they are listed after the world's built-in route, whose id is `world`, and offered in the start menu. There is no route editor yet.

Driving: W/S throttle and brake, A/D steer, Space handbrake. The handbrake locks the rear wheels: they slide with 70 % of the tyre grip (`HANDBRAKE_GRIP` in `car.gd`) against the direction they move, take no drive and hold the car at a standstill, so the rear swings out in a hairpin; let go when the car points out of the bend and steer into the slide with throttle. The start menu offers the routes of the world (its own route, named after its lap start, then the user routes) and Free drive (T or a click switches; the chosen route shows as a red line on the map). With a route chosen, start 1 starts it with the lap timer (the world's own route on its lap start, a user route on its first point), and the route stays in the world on the other starts too, with its roads closed off; Free drive shows no route and runs no lap timer. The menu lists the lap start only through the route. It also offers eight cars (`cars.gd`, C or a click switches, two columns in the menu), shown under made-up names (Rally Coupé Turbo, Rally Coupé 20V, Rally Coupé Short, Wedge V10, Boxer Saloon Turbo, Hot Hatch 16V, Twin Turbo V8, Flat Six Track; no makes, models or badges, see [licences.md](./licences.md#vehicles)) and modelled on: the Audi quattro (Typ 85, 1980-1991) as 2.1 Turbo WR (200 PS, the default) and as 20V RR (220 PS), both 4.40 m long, 1.72 m wide, 2.525 m wheelbase, 205/60 R 15; the Audi Sport quattro (306 PS) with the 2.224 m wheelbase (30 cm shorter; some sources give 2.204 m), the steeper windscreen and 235/45 R 15; the Lamborghini Huracán STO (640 PS V10 without turbo, rear-wheel drive, 7-speed dual clutch, 2.62 m wheelbase, 1.94 m wide, rear wing, down force); the Subaru Impreza WRX STI (GD, 265 PS boxer turbo, four-wheel drive split 35/65, 6 gears, bonnet scoop and boot wing); the VW Golf II GTI 16V (139 PS, front-wheel drive, the only one); the Ferrari F40 (478 PS twin-turbo V8 with the largest turbo lag, mid engine, rear-wheel drive, 5 gears); and the Porsche 911 GT3 RS (992, 525 PS flat six to 9000/min, engine behind the rear axle, 7-speed dual clutch, 409 kg down force at 200 km/h). Each has its torque curve, turbo lag where it has a turbo, a box that shifts by itself and its own engine sound (five-cylinder warble, V10, boxer rumble, four, flat-plane V8 or flat six); from the route start they do 0-100 km/h in 6.8 / 6.2 / 4.9 / 3.2 / 5.2 / 7.5 / 4.4 / 3.0 s (data sheets 7.1 / 6.3 / 4.9 / 3.0 / 5.5 / 8.5 / 4.6-4.7 / 3.2 s); on the flat the Impreza, the Golf and the F40 reach 242, 209 and 322 km/h (data sheets 244, 208, 321-324). The STO carries its centre of mass 0.2 m behind the middle (about 58 % on the driven rear axle). The data sheets give the peaks of power and torque, dimensions, tyres and 0-100; the torque between the peaks, the Audi track widths and final drive, two STO gear ratios, the F40 gears and final drive and the 911 gears and final drive are estimates, see the comment in `cars.gd`. The HUD shows speed, gear, engine speed and car. The world is in metres (UTM 32N), so the speedometer reads true: driven distance and integrated speed agree within 0.02 %. R puts the car back on the road: on a route start onto the nearest route point (the lap keeps running), on the other starts back onto the chosen start. V (gamepad RB) switches the camera between far behind, close behind and the driver's seat (`chase_cam.gd`; fixed to the car, body and wheels hidden, no dashboard). A click on the start menu's map starts on the nearest road there (x/z only, `road_index.nearest(..., flat)`), R then returns to that point. The minimap at the bottom right (`minimap.gd`) draws the roads of `roads.bin` within 600 m, turned with the view, motorways blue, trunk and primary roads yellow, secondary white, the rest grey (the fifth column of `roads.bin` is the road rank). # shows a position readout top centre, keeps it current while it shows (every 0.2 s, so a screenshot carries the spot it was taken at) and copies it to the clipboard once on the key press: world id, the nearest road from `road_names.json` with its distance, lat/lon (a plane approximation from the manifest centre, good to metres), true heading and pitch, the camera view, and the camera as a viewpoint (`{"position": …, "look_at": …}` in local metres, the format of `godot/tools/viewpoints*.json`), so a screenshot with that line can be rendered again. The compass and the readout turn with `heading_deg` in a turned world. Signs come from `pipeline/signs.py` as `signs.json` (drawn by `signs.gd`: posts, unshaded panels, one `Label3D` per line, text up to 250 m, no collision); the module doc is the full rule set. Destination signs and gantries come from the OSM `destination`, `destination:ref`, `destination:ref:to` and `destination:lanes` tags on roads from tertiary up and on links: a side sign 20 m after the start of the way with up to three destinations behind an arrow straight on and the first ref, or, on through carriageways (motorway, trunk, a oneway primary road with two lanes or more), a gantry with its panels 5 m above the road, one per group of lanes with the same destinations. Plain direction signs fill in where no destination sign stands within 400 m: before junctions of three or more arms from tertiary up, naming per arm the most important place within 30° and 1.5 to 40 km, with an arrow, at least 900 m apart. Town signs stand at the OSM town entry signs (`traffic_sign` city_limit, DE:310, FR:EB10), facing the traffic that enters the town (the side with more urban land cover and buildings); a place without a mapped town sign within 1.5 km gets one where a road enters urban land cover near it. Distance signs stand 120 m past a town sign for the traffic leaving it, on roads from tertiary up and 1.5 km apart: the nearest place ahead and the nearest bigger one with the road distance in km (shortest paths through the road graph; a place is ahead when its shortest path leaves in the travel direction). The style follows the country of the world (`country` in `signs.json`, from the Geofabrik path) and the road the sign stands on: in Germany yellow with black text on roads with a B, L or K ref and for all plain and distance signs, white in towns, blue on motorways, town signs yellow with a black border; in France white, green on N and T routes, blue on motorways, town signs white with a red border and the name in capitals. Side signs stand with the inner edge of their panel 0.6 m beyond the carriageway or its sidewalk, so no panel reaches over the road; wide panels stand on two posts. A panel that would cut into a building moves up to 20 m along the road or is left out. Arrows are geometry (the built-in font has no arrow glyphs). Stuttgart has 278 destination signs (58 gantries), 25 plain, 105 town and 8 distance signs; Berglen 30, 15, 88 and 15; Corte–Aléria 21 plain, 42 town and 6 distance signs; Bavella 3, 17 and 4 (Corsica has no destination tags in OSM). F puts the car onto the nearest road (gamepad X): `build.py` writes the drawn centre lines of all roads as `roads.bin` (every 5 m sample as float32 x, y, z in the glTF frame, y the drawn road surface with bridges and tunnels, plus the edge index; listed as `roads` in the manifest and packaged), `road_index.gd` keeps them in a 50 m grid and finds the nearest segment in 3D, so under a bridge the car lands on the road below and on a bridge on the deck; the car stands on the centre line, along the road, facing the way it was heading. Within 2 km nothing found, or a world without `roads.bin` (an older package): F puts the car back on its wheels where it is, up to 8 m to the side or turned if a building or wall is in the way; buildings are on their own collision layer, so the car is never put onto a roof. A compass top right turns with the view and shows the heading in degrees. Esc opens the start menu; Esc again resumes.

Flying (`make fly`, `fly_cam.gd`): WASD or the arrow keys move, Q/E down and up, Shift four times faster, the mouse wheel changes the speed. The mouse is captured as in a first-person shooter and moving it looks around; Esc releases it, a click captures it again, and while it is released a right mouse drag still looks. Gamepad: left stick moves, right stick looks, the triggers go down and up. 1-9 jump to the route waypoints, # shows the position readout.

The smoke test (`make test W=<id>`, or `-- --world <path>` for any directory or package) turns the menu off, drives from the route start, settles the car on every start point, moves it 25 m off the road, rolls it and checks that F puts it back upright and, with `roads.bin`, within 1 m of the nearest road's centre line. Then come the world's own runs from `worlds/<id>/smoke.json`: `tunnel` (start index, speed, timeout, maximum rise and a path in Godot x/z) and `bridge` (speed, timeout, maximum sink and a path in x/y/z with the road surface height). Stuttgart holds the two runs that used to be constants in the script: from the city centre start through the tunnel under the Gebhard-Müller-Platz at 40 km/h, where the car must reach the exit, stay upright and climb at most 3 m, and from the Cannstatter Straße east over the König-Karls-Brücke at 40 km/h, where it must reach the end, stay upright and never drop more than 1 m below the road surface. Last every car of the menu settles on the route start and must reach 100 km/h within 12 s at full throttle without tipping; the times are printed. The 0-50 and 30-0 km/h times and the load time are compared with `godot/tests/reference_<id>.json` (`SMOKE_WRITE_REFERENCE=1` writes it for the current world). A world without `smoke.json`, without a reference or without a route still runs the start-point and upright checks and prints `SKIPPED` for the rest, so a new world gets a useful test before it gets its own paths and reference.

## Screenshots without a GPU

`make render` renders fixed viewpoints of the fly scene to PNG on a machine without a graphics card, for example to attach before/after images to a merge request. Godot runs in a container under Xvfb with Mesa llvmpipe (software OpenGL 4.5) and the Compatibility renderer, so the images differ from the Forward+ build on a GPU: no sun shadows by default (with shadows llvmpipe draws a stipple pattern and washes out the colours), less haze towards the horizon and harder colour edges. They show terrain, land cover, roads, buildings and the route; for look and feel compare with the GPU image in the [spike document](./docs/spikes/2026-10-20_19-45_prototype-spikes.md#visual-check).

Prerequisites are docker and a built world (default `data/out/worlds/stuttgart/`, see above; `RENDER_WORLD` picks another). Build the image once (about 540 MB), then render:

```bash
# On Host
make build
make render                       # or ./render/render.sh
RENDER_NO_SHADOWS=0 RENDER_RESOLUTION=1920x1080 ./render/render.sh
```

Environment: `RENDER_RESOLUTION` (default `1280x720`), `RENDER_NO_SHADOWS` (default `1`, `0` turns the sun shadows on), `RENDER_TIMEOUT` (seconds, default 1800), `RENDER_VIEWS` (viewpoint file as `res://` path, default `res://tools/viewpoints.json`; only `godot/` is mounted into the container), `RENDER_IMAGE`, `RENDER_WORLD` (a world directory or a `.world` file, relative to the repository root unless absolute; default `data/out/worlds/stuttgart`). On a host whose docker daemon cannot see `/tmp` (a daemon of its own or a remote one), the world must live under a path the daemon can mount, such as `data/out/`. With `RENDER_WORLD` a build of the unchanged main branch, for example in `data/out/worlds/stuttgart-main` (a second checkout of main with `make world` and its world directory copied there, or a `.world` package of it), renders the same views for before/after pictures:

```bash
# On Host
RENDER_VIEWS=res://tools/viewpoints_polish.json ./render/render.sh
RENDER_WORLD=data/out/worlds/stuttgart-main \
  RENDER_VIEWS=res://tools/viewpoints_polish.json ./render/render.sh
```

Each run writes `data/out/screenshots/<YYYY-MM-DD_hh-ii>/` with one PNG per view, `godot.log` (the full Godot output) and `report.txt` (renderer, wall time per view, peak memory, host load, image sizes). The exit code is Godot's. Because the host may be shared, `render.sh` refuses to start at a 1-minute load of 4 or more, below 1 GiB of available memory or while another render container runs, and limits the container to 2 CPUs and 3 GiB.

A viewpoint in `godot/tools/viewpoints.json` has a `name` (`[a-z0-9_-]+`, the file name) and either a `waypoint` index into the route waypoints of `world.json` with an optional `offset` (default `[0, 80, 120]`, like the 1-3 keys of the fly scene) or an absolute `position` plus `look_at`. The file holds the three route waypoints plus two absolute views for the next bridge and junction work, `otto-hirsch` (the Otto-Hirsch-Brücken over the Neckar) and `neckartor` (B14 Cannstatter Straße / Am Neckartor), each 60 m above and 90 m south of a point on the road surface, two views of the tunnel portal north of the city centre start, `innenstadt-portal` (on the ramp, 40 m before the portal) and `innenstadt-tunnel` (from above), and `koenig-karls-bruecke`, the bridge deck over the Neckar from above the river:

```json
{"name": "neckar", "position": [500, 150, 300], "look_at": [0, 0, 0]}
```

A skirt or a lane taper is invisible from 50-60 m, so `godot/tools/viewpoints_polish.json` holds 20 low (2-7 m above the road, 10-20 m back) and oblique (14-80 m) views: the road edges beside a sidewalk, on a slope and in a narrow median, a taper, junction corners, the Heilandsplatz roundabout, tunnel portals and tubes, steep and curved streets, two overviews, and the route band on the König-Karls-Brücke and in the Rosensteintunnel. `pipeline/scout.py` writes it from the world's scenes, `worlds/stuttgart/scenes.json`: a scene names one or two streets, a mode (`edge` a point on the street looking along it, `junction` the node where both streets meet or a roundabout centre, `median` the narrowest median of a dual carriageway, `portal` a tunnel portal, `taper` the largest width step, `route` inside the longest route run on a tunnel, `waypoint` a waypoint view), the camera height and distance and a one-sentence hint. The script builds the road network the way `build.py` does, so the heights are the drawn road surface, prints every scene with its local position and skips a scene whose street is not in the extract with a warning. The file is committed, so the pictures are reproducible without running the scout; after a change to the scenes or the road network:

```bash
uv run python pipeline/scout.py data/out/worlds/stuttgart/extract.pkl \
  --recipe worlds/stuttgart/world.json \
  --out godot/tools/viewpoints_polish.json
```

Centre, size and world id come from the recipe; `--scenes` defaults to `scenes.json` next to the recipe and `--world` (the built world, for its route and DGM1 file name) to `data/out/worlds/<id>`.

On the shared 4-vCPU host a 1280x720 image takes about 3-4 s with a peak of about 650 MiB (with shadows 5-7 s and about 1 GiB), a run of three views about 15 s including loading the world.

## Desktop build (Linux, Windows, macOS)

`make export` exports the game for the three desktop platforms into `./build/` (`make export-image` once before, and again after a Godot version change). No world is in the package: build one with `make world` (or `make pack` for a single `.world` file) and put it next to the executable.

`--world <path>` and `--world=<path>` after `--` are the same (so are `--start` and `--route` with `=`); the path is a world directory with `world.json` or a `.world` file. Without `--world` the world selection also lists every `.world` file and every world directory (such as `world/`) next to the executable, on macOS next to the `.app`. A drive or fly scene started without a world takes the first that exists of `world/` next to the executable, on macOS `world/` next to the `.app`, and, in the editor or when started from the checkout (`--path godot`), `data/out/worlds/stuttgart`. When the world does not open, the scene shows the reason and the searched paths.

```bash
# On Host
make export-image                 # once, about 550 MB
make export                       # build/Linux, build/Windows, build/macOS
./heimstrecke.x86_64 -- --world=/path/to/world           # a world directory
./heimstrecke.x86_64 -- --world /path/to/stuttgart-1.world  # or a package
```

The Windows build has no icon and version info (no `rcedit`), the macOS build is universal and signed ad-hoc only, without notarization. Remove the quarantine flag before the first start: `xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine "Heimstrecke.app"`.

### Route direction, start and finish, checkpoints

The start menu offers the direction of the chosen route (D or the button): from the route's first point to its last (for a route named "A – B" shown as "A → B") or the other way, also on a circuit; the route marks, gates and lap timer are built again for it. All start points of a world face the same way, along the route (`starts.py` turns a start without `toward` along the route), and are turned around when the route is driven backwards. The first and the last point of the route are the real starts: a black and white chequered banner on two posts over the road (`route_gates.gd`, labelled START and FINISH; on a circuit both stand at the same place), the route start button puts the car in front of the first one, and the stopwatch in the HUD (Time, the split of the last checkpoint, Last) runs from the first movement to the finish. Between them a blue checkpoint gate stands every 4 to 10 km: the route is cut into the fewest equal legs of at most 10 km (41 km: 5 legs of 8.2 km, 6 gates). The car has to pass each gate within 30 m. `--reverse` after `--` starts the route backwards. `make test-gates` checks the gate spacing.

Bavella has a route as well: the D 268 from Solenzara on the coast up to the Col de Bavella, 30.2 km (one OSRM response, `worlds/bavella/route.json`, waypoints in `route_waypoints.json`), with the checkpoints every 4 to 10 km (4 legs of 7.5 km, 5 gates). The recipe is checked (`world.py vars`), the world has not been rebuilt for it yet.
